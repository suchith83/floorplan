## 5. Result and why the two scans differ (written by hand, 7 Oct 2026)

**Gate not met.** Per wall within max(1 cm, 0.5 %): **0 / 16** wall-to-wall spans and **1 / 23** polygon edges.
The two scans are certainly the same flat (69 % of wall points within 5 cm after a 90.4° turn; the next-best
start gets 24 %), but dimensions repeat to roughly **±5 cm** (span median |Δ| 5.5 cm), not ±1 cm.

The same script with drift correction **off** in both scans (`--no-drift-correction`), and each scan against itself:

| comparison | reg. inliers | rooms matched | spans within gate | span median abs(Δ) | edge median abs(Δ) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a83 vs c7d2, drift **off** | 55 % | 6 / 8 | 3 / 12 | 1.9 cm | 39.8 cm |
| 1a83 vs c7d2, drift **on, step** (one correction per submap) | 69 % | 8 / 8 | 1 / 23 | 5.6 cm | 11.8 cm |
| 1a83 vs c7d2, drift **on, blended** (shipped) | 69 % | 6 / 9 | 0 / 16 | 5.5 cm | 12.5 cm |
| 1a83 off vs 1a83 on (same scan) | – | 8 / 8 | 3 / 11 | 5.5 cm | – |
| c7d2 off vs c7d2 on (same scan) | – | 8 / 8 | 7 / 26 | 2.3 cm | – |

What the table says:
1. **Drift correction makes the flat globally consistent.** More of one scan's walls land on the other's (55 → 69 %),
   rooms match one-to-one more often, polygon edges agree 3× better (40 → 12 cm), and within each scan about
   half of the double walls disappear (see `out/<id>/debug/drift_ablation.png`).
2. **It costs a few cm inside rooms.** Each submap gets one rigid correction from ICP loop closures that are
   themselves good to ~1–1.5 cm RMSE, and the pose graph spreads their disagreements. ARKit on its own is
   locally excellent (a room scanned in one pass is self-consistent to millimetres), so a span measured in
   one pass repeats well with drift off (1.9 cm), while corrected submaps move a room's opposite walls by
   2–6 cm relative to each other (same-scan rows). Blending corrections in time instead of one step per
   submap did not change this (5.6 vs 5.5 cm), so the error is in the corrections, not in the jumps between them.
3. **Segmentation dominates room widths/areas.** Differences of 0.8–1.3 m in section 2 are a doorway, alcove or
   corridor piece assigned to a different room in each scan, not measurement error: the matched wall planes
   themselves sit a median 3.8 cm apart.

Why the two captures differ physically:
- **Wall coverage.** `1a8384c3f6` is 115 s with the phone pitched down (no ceiling: highest point 2.35 m);
  `c7d28f72c6` is 215 s, includes a ceiling pass and revisits rooms. Different walls are seen, from different
  distances, and furniture hides different stretches; a wall plane is fitted to whatever was seen.
- **The ceiling pass** gives c7d2 more frames per room and more loop closures (17 accepted vs 8), so its
  correction is better constrained (largest correction 21 cm / 1.2° vs 68 cm / 6.9° for 1a83, most of which
  is a rigid turn of the whole map that alignment removes).
- **Residual drift.** After correction, accepted loops still disagree by ~2 cm RMSE (c7d2 3.22 → 2.12 cm,
  1a83 3.34 → 1.83 cm), the same order as the span differences.

Nothing here is ground truth; it says how repeatable the pipeline is, not how accurate.
